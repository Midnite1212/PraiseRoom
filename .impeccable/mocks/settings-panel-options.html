Praise Room

Text & chord settings: options

Each layout shows both panels, Aa (text) and ⚙ (chords), as they would open on a phone over the song. Every control works: tap around, and the lyrics behind the glass change with it. Icons do the talking; hover or long-press any of them for its name.

  • Same settings in all four: text size, split or one song, auto-scroll, keep awake, theme, language, Present to TV · notation, simple chords, colour chords, sharps or flats, capo
O

Original

Today's panels, as they open on a phone: a row per setting, a label on the left, worded choices on the right, and a grey hint under some labels.

O+

Original, lighter new

The same panels, rows and labels. The choices become icons where an icon is clear, and short marks where it isn't; the hint lines go.

  • Nothing to relearn: same rows, same order, same labels.
  • About half the words, and the panels get shorter.
  • Speed dials and the theme's "auto" icon rely on their tooltips the first time.
  • Language and sharps or flats stay as short text; no icon says them better.

On a desktop: O+ and E

On wide screens each panel is a 360px popover that grows from the button you pressed, with no dimming. The Reading panel runs top-down and adds Layout (split or one song). Click Aa or the gear in the top row.

A

Live previews

Every choice is drawn as its own result: text sizes at their real size, layouts as little pages, chord settings as the chords themselves.

  • Nothing to read: you see what you'll get before you tap.
  • Chord settings make sense to non-musicians too.
  • Both choices of each setting stay on screen, so it's the tallest panel.
  • Theme, language and keep awake still need icons; they can't preview.
B

Pixel tiles

A grid of square tiles, one per setting. A tile lights up when it's on; a row of pixels shows levels like scroll speed. Text size is a pixel meter.

  • The most "Praise Room": state is light, like the setlist pixels.
  • Compact and even; one tap per setting.
  • A tile shows only its current state, so cycling (Off → Slow → Med → Fast) takes several taps.
  • Needs a one-word caption under each icon to stay clear.
C

Glass ribbon

No sheet at all: one row of icon buttons grows out of the button you tapped, and a small label names whatever you just changed.

  • The lightest; almost the whole song stays visible.
  • Fast for people who know the icons.
  • Fits about nine controls; buttons shrink to 40px.
  • Meaning lives in the icons and the label, so it's the hardest to learn.
D

Mix recommended

A's live chord line, B's tiles and pixel meter, and the set-once settings (theme, language, keep awake) tucked into a row of small icons at the foot.

  • The things you change mid-song are big; the things you set once are small.
  • The chord line explains every chord tile, so the tiles need no hints.
  • Two visual styles in one panel (tiles and a preview line).
  • Theme and language are a little less discoverable at the foot.
E

Icons with labels new

D's structure, but every control pairs its icon with a short name and its current setting ("Auto-scroll · Slow"). No hint sentences; the chord line still shows the result.

  • Readable at a glance: icon to find it, words to be sure.
  • Each control says its current state, so nothing has to be guessed.
  • Wider controls: two per row instead of three or four.
  • A little more text than D, though far less than today's panels.
